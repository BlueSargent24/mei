## Geometrical crystallography (external)

### Crystal Construction
**Pauling (1929) cation-anion 5 rules**
1. A polyhedron of anions is formed about each cation
   distance = radii sum
   number of coordinated anions in the polyhedron is determined by the radius ratio
2. **electrostatic valency rule**: an ionic structure is stable when the sum of the strengths of all the bonds balances the charge on the cation and the anions
3. sharing of edges and faces by adjacent anion polyhedral elements decreases the stability of an ionic structure
4. Cations with high valence charges and small CN tend not to share polyhedral elements. Positive charges tend to repel.
5. **Rule of parsimony**: the number of different kinds of constituents (like cation and anion sites) in a crystal structure tends to be small

> [!note] larger radius ratio = higher CN

#### Polymorphic Reactions

1. **Displacive** - minor adjustments to the structure
   - bond kinking; fast; easily reversible
1. **Order-Disorder** - one polymorph has complete ordering; another is random
2. **Reconstructive** - reaction requires bond breaking and reassembly of bonds; requires lots of energy; slow; almost irreversible

#### Solid Solution
- substitution of one ion for another in a crystal lattice
- produces minerals with the same crystal structure but different compositions
- ions with similar ionic radius and charge substitute for one another in a crystal structure

==**If size difference between ions is <15% substitution likely, 15%-30% limited, >30% not likely**==

Common ions involved in substitutions:
1.  Fe²⁺, Mg²⁺
2. Na⁺, Ca²⁺
3. Al³⁺, Si⁴⁺
 
==**Greater substitution possible at higher temperatures**==

**Coupled substitution**: if the substituting ions are of different charges, then more than one balance must be made

### Crystallographic Axes

**Importance**
- tool for studying crystals
- give relation between crystal faces
- recognize crystal system

**Characteristics**
- straight lines
- intersect at a point called axial cross
- 3 or 4
- one is vertical, the rest are horizontal
- may be equal in length or different

### Parameters
intersection of the faces with the crystallographic axes
relative distance where faces intercept the axes

### Miller Indices
**reciprocal of parameters**

1. determine the parameters
2. inverting the parameters
3. clearing the fractions

the Miller Index is written inside parentheses with no commas, ex. (110)

examples:

| Face | a     | b   | c        | Parameters         | Reciprocal         | Miller Index |
| ---- | ----- | --- | -------- | ------------------ | ------------------ | ------------ |
|      | $1$   | $1$ | $1$      | $1a:1b:1c$         | $1/1:1/1:1/1$      | $(111)$      |
|      | $3/2$ | $3$ | $\infty$ | $3/2a:3b:\infty c$ | $2/3:1/3:1/\infty$ | $(210)$      |
