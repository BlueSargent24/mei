Pleistocene volcanism in Mindoro is represented by volcanic centers such as Mt. Dumali and Mt. Macapili and other areas. The volcanism is related to the subduction of the South China Sea Plate along the southern trace of the Manila Trench that impinges on Mindoro. Other areas where such volcanism had taken place are Eplog Hill, Pola, Maestre de Campo and Simara islands off northern Mindoro, Calapan, Lake Naujan; and Mauhao. The volcanic rocks consist mostly of andesites, except at Mauhao where basalt is also present, together with pyroxene andesite (MMAJ-JICA, 1984). Radiometric K-Ar dating of samples from Mt. Macapili gave values of 1.56 – 1.64 Ma while a sample from Mt. Dumali gave a value of 0.82 Ma (De Boer and others, 1980).

The Dumali is probably equivalent to the [Eplog lava flows](Eplog%20Lava%20Flows.md) of Weller and Vergara (1955) named after Mt. Eplog, the highest hill in Balatasan Peninsula, southeastern Mindoro. The Eplog consists of lava flows with a thickness of at least 35 meters. Similar flows occur near Akihit at the mouth of Naujan Valley. The lava consists of vesicular and glassy hornblende andesite. Some of the vesicles are partly filled with calcite and zeolite.
