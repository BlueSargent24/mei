  

In association with the Lumao Diabase in Pujada Pennsula are cross cutting dikes of hydrothermally altered basalt, diabase and gabbro designated informally by Villamor and others (1984) as Lungag Dike Complex for the exposures at Lungag Creek and the upper reaches of Luzon River. It is considered part of the Pujada Ophiolite. Diabase is the dominant lithology of the complex. The upper portion is made up of hydrothermally metamorphosed diabase and basalt. The lower portion of the Lungag Dike Complex extends to the upper portion of the Matalao gabbro. The Complex can be traced along a north-northwest direction for about 19 km with a width ranging from 200 m to 3 km. The thickness of the dikes ranges from a few centimeters to a meter. Dike contacts are sharp and characterized by chilled margins. The dikes trend NE-SW and dip steeply to the southeast. (_see [Lumao Diabase](Lumao%20Diabase.md)_)
