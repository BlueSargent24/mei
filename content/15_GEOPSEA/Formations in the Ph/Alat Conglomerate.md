  

The Alat Conglomerate crops out along Sapang Alat, about 3 km north of the Novaliches Reservoir and forms an extensive outcrop belt underlying the hills and lowlands in eastern Bulacan and southeastern Nueva Ecija. The Alat is a sequence of conglomerate, sandstone and mudstones that forms the lower member of the Guadalupe Formation. The conglomerate, which is the most predominant rock type, is massive, poorly sorted with well-rounded pebbles and small boulders of older rocks – diorite, gabbro, basalt, andesite and limestone -- cemented by coarse grained, calcareous and sandy matrix. The interbedded sandstone is massive to poorly bedded, tuffaceous, fine to medium grained, loosely cemented, friable and exhibits cross bedding. The mudstone is medium to thin bedded, soft, silty and tuffaceous. The maximum estimated thickness of this member is 200 m. (_See [Guadalupe Formation](Guadalupe%20Formation.md)_)
