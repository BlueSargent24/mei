## Anisotropic vs Isotropic Minerals
- based on: 
	- light ray travelling through and mineral interaction
	- chemical bonds nature

### Isotropic
- same velocity of light in all directions
- [[Isometric System]]
- Wave Normal and Light Ray are parallel
- always black in XPL
- light does not get rotated or split

### Anisotropic
- different velocity for light; direction-dependent
- [[Tetragonal System|Tetragonal]], [[Monoclinic System|Monoclinic]], [[Hexagonal System|Hexagonal]], [[Triclinic System|Triclinic]], [[Trigonal System|Trigonal]], [[Orthorhombic System|Orthorhombic]]
- not parallel wave normal and light ray
- capable of reorienting light

**Polarized light** - vibrates in a single plane at any one moment in time; direction of the plane of vibration changes with time (plane polarized)

## Mineral properties in PPL
### Color
- only in PPL
- changes with light type/intensity
- results from selective absorption of certain $\lambda$ of light

### Pleochroism
- when different $\lambda$ are absorbed differently by different crystallographic direction
- pleochroic minerals change color when rotated
- fast and slow rays are perpendicular to each other; when one ray vibrates parallel to the lower polarizer, all light passes as that ray, and then the mineral will display the color of that ray
- if *both* rays come through, color is an *intermediate* between them

**double refraction** - splitting of a light mean into two perpendicularly polarized rays

### Refraction Index
- $n=\frac{V_{air}}{V_{mineral}}$
- function of crystallographic orientation
	- isotropic - one RI
	- uniaxial - two RI
	- biaxial - three RI
- refraction accompanied by velocity change
#### Refractometry
- determination of the refractive index of minerals
- easiest is immersion method

Immersion oils of known RI is used to compare the unknown mineral to the oil
- to compare, relief and Becke Line are used
	- Relief for estimate values
	- Becke Line for determining positive or negative relief
	- balsam - n=1.540
### Relief
- measure of the relative difference in n between a mineral grain and surroundings
- to estimate n
Difference:
Strong relief - > 0.12 RI
Moderate relief - 0.04 - 0.12 RI
Low relief - <=0.04 RI

### Becke Line
- band along the grain boundary when slightly out of focus
- minerals can act either as diverging or converging lenses
- form at mineral-epoxy, mineral-mineral boundaries
- they always move into higher RI material when the stage is lowered

**Internal Reflection Effect**
- why Becke Lines form
- with the light hitting the grain boundary, the light is either refracted or internally reflected
- the combined result of refraction and IR concentrates light into a thin band in the material of HIGHER refractive index (the light chooses the higher)

**Microscope**
- use medium or high power, close aperture diagram
- the direction of movement of the Line is determined by lowering the stage with the Line always moving into the material with the HIGHER refractive index

- when dispersion curves for min and oil intersect - formation of 2 Becke Lines, matching of RI for only one wavelength of light each

## XPL
- minerals reorient the planes in which light is vibrating
- most grains change color as the stage is rotated

### Extinction
- many grains go dark every 90° of rotation
- causes:
	- occurs when principle vibration directions are parallel to vibration directions of upper and lower polarizers
	- light retains original polarized direction
	- light blocked by analyzer
- allows determination of principle vibration directions
- when extinct, orientation of the principle vibration directions are N-S and E-W
### Extinction Angle
- angle between the length or cleavage in a mineral and a vibration direction

steps:
1. rotate the stage until the length/cleavage is aligned with the north-south crosshair
2. record the reading from the stage goniometer (g1)
3. rotate the stage until it goes extinct
4. record the new reading (g2)
EA is the difference between g1 and g2

- EA measured on grains that display max retardation
### Sign of Elongation
- refers to whether the mineral is length fast or length slow

|                                         | Length fast             | Length slow             |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| parallel to the *length* of the mineral | fast ray                | slow ray                |
| sign of elongation                      | *negative elongation* − | *positive elongation* + |
*How do we know?*
1. start with the mineral extinct and with the mineral elongation, must be less than 45° from the N-S crosshairs (position after rotating to measure the EA)
2. rotate 45° clockwise; vibration direction closest to the length or prominent cleavage NE-SW
3. note interference color
4. insert accessory plate
	retardations add - length slow
	retardations subtract - length fast

### Accessory Plates
- gypsum and mica plates
- produce a known amount of retardation and the slow ray vibration is across the width of the holder and the fast ray vibration direction is across the length of the holder
- placed usually at NW-SE so slow vibrates NE-SW, and fast ray NW-SE

Function:
- determine optic sign
- determine sign of elongation

#### Gypsum plate
- full wave plate: 537 or 550 nm of retardation
- magenta interference color seen at the transition from 1st to 2nd order

#### Mica plate
- 1/4 wave plate: 150 nm of retardation and yields a 1st order white interference color

### Interference Phenomena
**Interference color** - specific color passing through the analyzer; two rays resolving to one ray

### Birefringence
- difference between the RIs of slow and fast ray
- $\delta=(n_s - n_f)$
### Retardation
- controlled by thin section thickness and birefringence
- $Δ =  t ⨉ δ'$; t is thickness of the crystal. units in nm
- simply the observed interference color, Δ
	- sum of wavelengths that are half integers of retardation

### Color Chart
Two primary uses:
1. determine birefringence of mineral
2. determine thickness of thin sections

- divided into orders in multiples of 550 nm

| c-axis to stage      | extinction           | birefringence | retardation |
| -------------------- | -------------------- | ------------- | ----------- |
| parallel             | parallel             | maximum       | maximum     |
| perpendicular (*OA*) | behaves as isotropic | 0             | 0           |
| intermediate         |                      |               |             |


#### Determining thickness
- find mineral (quartz for example) with highest birefringence
	- scan many quartz crystals
- find where the retardation intersects lines for birefringence (δ = 0.009)
- calculate it from formula d = Δ/δ, or read from chart (that would be 0.03 most likely)

#### Determining birefringence
- find grain with highest interference colors (have the fastest and slowest n values)
- find retardation based on color
- calculate using δ = Δ/d, or find from chart

## Optic Axis
Quadrant labels
- labeled starting in the upper right and going counterclockwise; uses roman numerals
- optic axis - crystallographic Z or C axis
- when a thin section of a mineral is cut perpendicular to the OA, then viewed perpendicular to the thin section, light is travelling along the optic axis
- light travelling in this direction experiences a single RI (n), ω

### OA Figures
- isogyre has 1° color; the area between the arms is 1° white, unless isochromes are present
- ω always vibrates parallel to the z axis an tangential to the isochromes, where
- **melatope** - optic axis itself; point where the isogyres meet
- isochromes
	- light passing around the melatope experience moderate retardation, going farther the melatope > retardation
		- because light makes a larger angle with optic axis and must take a longer path through the sample

# Dumping
**PPL** implications of double refraction
- double refraction - ordinary and extraordinary rays
- In my understanding, anisotropic minerals have the ability to split polarized light passing through it into two rays namely, fast and slow ray. They vibrate perpendicular to each other
- In plane polarized light, this causes the mineral to exhibit *pleochroism* wherein the mineral grain changes the perceived color upon rotation. 
- they travel in different velocities
- slow ray RI > fast ray; slow - fast = birefringence
- retardation Δ =  t ⨉ δ'; t is thickness of the crystal
**XPL** implications
- determining the respective colors of the fast and slow ray
- if light ray is vibrating parallel to the optic axis (special section of minerals), the anisotropic mineral will behave like an isotropic mineral

Maximum birefringence & retardation when c axis is parallel to stage; extinction nope
